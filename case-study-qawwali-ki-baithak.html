01 / Concept

A different visual identity for a timeless sound.

“Qawwali Ki Baithak is a personal reinterpretation of an existing album cover. I wanted to create a visual identity that felt closer to the atmosphere of traditional Qawwali — something archival, intimate and rooted in musical heritage.”

“The direction developed around aged paper, monochromatic imagery and deep-red typography. I wanted the design to feel like an old printed music archive while still having a distinct visual personality.”

* Note: This is an unofficial personal concept created to explore visual redesign in Adobe Photoshop, and is not officially affiliated with or commissioned by the artists.

Distressed red typewriter typography on aged fibrous paper.

02 / Visual Direction

The visual language

Key design decisions developed to ground the release in archival authenticity, tactile warmth and reverent minimalism.

MATERIAL

Texture

Warm, aged paper gives the design an archival, printed feel. The visible pulp, fibers and organic tooth transform the digital canvas into a tactile historical artifact.

TYPE

Typography

Distressed, typewriter-inspired red lettering creates character and contrast. Subtle ink bleeds, stenciled wear and imperfect letterforms evoke mid-century print recordings.

SUBJECT

Imagery

Monochromatic portraits and traditional musical instruments connect the artwork to its musical roots. High-contrast halftones honour the classical masters Fareed Ayaz & Abu Muhammad.

STRUCTURE

Composition

Minimal layouts and generous negative space allow each element to stand out. Balanced margins give breathing room to the central musical instruments.

04 / Details

Typography and details

Macro inspections of the handcrafted design components, ink bleeds and typographic hierarchy created in Adobe Photoshop.

TITLE LETTERING

Main Album Title

Spacious lowercase sans-serif lettering with generous tracking across the upper third of the artwork.

TYPEWRITER STAMP

Distressed Red Typography

Texture-integrated typewriter print with micro-distress in rich archival crimson ink (#A71916).

EDITORIAL BILLING

Artist Names Billing

Understated dual-corner credit anchors: “Fareed Ayaz” anchored left and “Abu Muhammad” anchored right.

HERITAGE ARTWORK

Traditional Instruments

Duo tablas, dholak and harmonium rendered in fine halftone stippling, representing classical Qawwal ensemble.

ARCHIVAL ARCHITECTURE

Portrait & Curatorial Text Layout

A companion layout documenting the lineage of the Qawwal Bachchon Ka Gharana, pairing monochromatic portraits with red typewriter biography and “est. 1989” provenance stamp.

What I explored

I experimented with an archival visual direction and learned to build a more consistent visual language across different artwork formats.”
